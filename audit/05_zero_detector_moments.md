# Part 5 — Zero Detector and Moment Estimates

**Status:** Analysis of residual-row control via truncated inverses and moment inductions. Saturation correctly forces the length relation that feeds the envelope; residual risk remains in the precise size of the induction losses.

## 5.1 Zero detector construction

For each residual sixth-power-free row $u$, a finite family of Hecke twists $\mathcal{X}_u$ is formed. A buffered bin $(i,a)$ is assigned according to the maximal real part of zeros of these twists inside a height-bounded rectangle ($T_1=Z^\tau$, $\tau$ small). If $a>51/100$, a zero exists above the floor.

A classical truncated-inverse construction (Möbius coefficients plus a Gamma-integral profile) then produces two Dirichlet polynomials sharing the same row character and twist height:

- an inverse polynomial $M_r$ (truncated reciprocal),
- a plain polynomial $S_m$.

When a zero is present, both are large. Proposition on detector-witness asserts simultaneous lower bounds
$$

|M_r S_m|^2\gg U^{\delta(r+m)-\epsilon},\qquad |M_r|^2\gg U^{\delta r-\epsilon},\qquad |S_m|^2\gg U^{\delta m-\epsilon}
$$
(with $\delta=2a-1\ge1/50$) together with the length relation $r+m\ge t-O(1/\log U)$.

## 5.2 Saturation and the length relation

Pointwise upper bounds (from the zero-free rectangle and reflection) give
$$

|M_r|^2\ll U^{\delta r+\epsilon},\qquad |S_m|^2\ll U^{\delta\min(m,1-m)+\epsilon}.
$$
Comparing the product lower bound with the product of the individual upper bounds forces
$$
\delta\bigl(m-\min(m,1-m)\bigr)\ll\epsilon.
$$
Since $\delta\ge1/50$, this implies the key length relation
$$
m\le\tfrac12+O(\epsilon).
$$
The inverse witness is therefore the longer one. This is the standard zero-detector mechanism; no structural defect is apparent.

## 5.3 Moment inductions

**Inverse polynomials.** A second-moment bound is proved by induction on row length. Two finite Poisson transformations shorten the range; the terminal bound is supplied by reflected energy. The induction hypotheses require a positive margin between the current length and the target length; each step loses a controlled amount (order $\eta$ or $\tau$) that is reserved in advance.

**Plain polynomials.** A mean-square estimate is obtained by ordinary Hecke reflection followed by the same style of Poisson shortening. Rows whose primitive inducing character lies in a fixed finite group (generated by the target and auxiliary ray characters) are excluded or handled separately; principal characters are excluded when no extra prime factors are present.

Both inductions produce the moment bounds needed to feed the sextic large sieve on the squarefree factor of the physical rows, yielding the envelope $R(\delta)$ of Part 4.

## 5.4 How the detector feeds the margin

The saturated inverse polynomial of controlled length supplies the coefficient sequence to which the specialized sextic sieve is applied. The resulting row count $\#\mathcal{B}\ll U^{R(\delta)+\epsilon}$ is strictly better than the trivial count in the operating range. When this count is inserted into the Poisson-side estimate, the residual contribution is smaller than the principal signal by a positive power — the $\sigma$ of the continuation criterion.

## 5.5 Potential fragility points

1. **Saturation length calculation.** The comparison that forces $m\le1/2+O(\epsilon)$ relies on the pointwise bounds holding with the stated exponents. An error in the reflected bound or in the deletion of Euler factors could weaken the upper bound on $S_m$ and destroy the length relation.

2. **Induction losses.** Each Poisson step in the moment inductions loses a small amount of margin. The paper reserves a budget for these losses and asserts that the terminal bound still leaves a positive remainder. Because the final margins are themselves small, an under-counted loss at any induction step can erase $\sigma$.

3. **Height factors.** The detector rectangles and the late-height closure must absorb the factors $(1+T_1)^{A_\eta}$. The parameter ordering ($\tau$ chosen after the height order $A_\eta$) is designed to make this possible; it must be verified that the ordering is respected throughout.

## 5.6 Condensed finding

The zero detector is the standard truncated-inverse construction and correctly forces the length relation that makes the inverse witness dominant. The moment inductions are set up to deliver the second-moment and mean-square bounds required by the sextic sieve. No structural defect is identified. The residual risk is quantitative: the induction losses and height factors must be strictly smaller than the modest positive margin produced by the sieve envelope. The argument survives this layer pending verification of those ledgers.
